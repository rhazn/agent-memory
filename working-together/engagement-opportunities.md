## Post Selection

- Select posts from the past week. Prefer more recent posts.
- Prefer short, ordinary LinkedIn posts and questions over long articles or blog-style posts that require substantial reading before joining the conversation.
- Favor some existing engagement and room for a meaningful exchange. Avoid crowded threads with hundreds of comments and announcements with no discussion or engagement. Do not impose fixed engagement thresholds.
- Avoid engagement bait and authors whose visible activity is predominantly engagement bait.
- Topic relevance is not enough. Look for a substantive discussion rather than a product announcement with little room to respond.
- Teaching-style posts can be too blog-like even without an external article. Prefer ordinary conversations and concrete problems over polished explanatory posts.
- Exclude posts when the signed-in account cannot comment. If explaining the exclusion, state the actual restriction, such as "only the author's connections can comment," rather than calling it a "restricted discussion."
- Be cautious about topics outside Philip's expertise, especially culturally or politically sensitive discussions involving marginalized communities. Do not suggest confident contributions without sufficient context.

## Comment Voice

- Use plain, natural language and few specialist terms.
- Write somewhat longer, conversational comments with enough context to develop the point. Do not default to a terse one- or two-sentence question. Add substance, not filler; do not try to demonstrate expertise in every reply or impose a fixed length.
- Favor genuine curiosity, a focused question, a relevant observation, or a supported personal example.
- Engage primarily with the content of the post. Use author and company research to assess fit, not to make the comment sound like a researched sales approach.
- For unfamiliar authors, avoid pointed, complex questions about their specific business unless the post invites that depth. A straightforward business question can fit, but do not imply prior familiarity.
- Use concrete wording that makes the reasoning clear. Avoid compressed or abstract phrases that require the reader to infer the point.
- Allow genuine uncertainty and informal language. Do not replace a natural exchange with polished expert advice.
- Appreciation and thanks are welcome when sincere.
- Do not default to unsolicited tips or instructions. Share an idea as part of a conversation, not as a drive-by expert intervention.
- Do not force every comment into a question or a fixed template.
- Never invent experience, opinions, or familiarity.

## Opportunity Summaries

- Write longer post summaries, at least twice the length of the earlier one- or two-sentence summaries. Explain the situation, decisions and tensions, and highlight concrete questions or other hooks for responding.
- Omit descriptions of existing replies and statements about what was read. Keep those checks behind the scenes.
- Briefly explain any project, working group, standard, or initiative needed to understand the discussion.
- Base these explanations on the post and relevant linked sources. Read those sources rather than guessing from names.
- State missing context or access limits clearly.
