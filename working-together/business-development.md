## Engagement and Location

- Seek agency project engagements, including subcontracting for other agencies.
  Continue to exclude pure staff augmentation/body leasing and intermediary
  talent pools.
  Unverified direct-client status alone is not grounds for exclusion.
- Prefer concrete agency-compatible procurement evidence. When stronger leads
  are absent, also consider briefs seeking a freelancer to implement a concrete
  project or deliverable. Isselthal's small-company model can support a
  freelancer-style engagement; an explicit request for an agency is not required.
  Distinguish project delivery from selling ongoing individual capacity within
  a client team. Technical relevance alone does not justify body leasing.
- Strongly favor early-stage startups and small companies where Philip can work
  directly with the founder and deliver clearly bounded work packages. Helping an
  individual owner build or improve their product is an especially strong fit.
  This is a particularly attractive delivery model, not staff augmentation merely
  because the founder remains hands-on. Assess freshness, project size, and explicit
  application requirements separately from this positive engagement fit.
- "No agencies or intermediaries" is a caveat to highlight, not an automatic
  exclusion. As a very small company, Isselthal may be able to offer the work
  directly through the individual who delivers it. Do not assume acceptance or
  conceal the contracting entity.
- Exclude permanent employee roles, including interesting roles that
  could only hypothetically become consulting work. A fixed-term employment
  contract is not a freelance signal.
- Before each agency search, inspect, set, and verify the portal filters. Philip
  also uses these accounts for personal job searches, so saved preferences are
  not an agency-search preset. Use Freelance only for contract type, broad
  experience levels, and remote work or local work in Paris. Apply the separate
  Georg/Nuremberg and exceptional-travel rules below when relevant.
- Search Welcome to the Jungle and Freelancermap
  (`https://www.freelancermap.de/projekte`), as well as the other current skill
  sources. On Freelancermap, configure and verify useful filters before reviewing
  the project results. Do not assume a project portal excludes intermediaries.
- On Freelancermap, favor direct-client projects with independent, bounded
  deliverables. Intermediary projects need a compelling reason to warrant a
  limited application slot; technical relevance alone is insufficient. Prefer
  fewer strong recommendations and a short non-recommended watchlist.
- Welcome to the Jungle is essential. Start at its homepage and navigate through
  the site. If sign-in is required, notify Philip and abort the search.
- For Philip individually, regular office attendance is acceptable only in Paris.
- For an Isselthal partnership with Georg Schwarz, Nuremberg (Nürnberg) is also
  acceptable. Georg is a collaborator, not a co-owner.
- Fully remote engagements are acceptable. Travel elsewhere is acceptable only

## Fit and Screening

- Exclude tiny engagements of only a few hours. Their contracting overhead,
  particularly an NDA, is not worthwhile.
- An NDA is a disadvantage, not a no-go for an otherwise strong product-delivery
  engagement. Assess it separately from the project's size and fit.
- A EUR 5,000 fixed-price budget can be acceptable currently, including for larger
  projects. Consider discussing implementation scope rather than excluding on
  budget alone.
- Requirements that could be met through company changes, such as employing
  Philip, are ranking disadvantages rather than automatic exclusions. Include
  otherwise relevant opportunities when stronger leads are absent, without
  assuming willingness to make those changes.
- Favor product-focused software engineering and qualitative or quantitative
  software-engineering research. Do not recommend roles requiring strong
  algorithmic-theory or C++ expertise.
- Exclude Windows-focused work, including Windows Server/IIS migrations. Philip
  does not want to work with Windows; relevant migration expertise is also absent.
- Exclude low-level server administration, distribution-specific Linux management,
  and server patch-management engagements. Neither Philip nor Georg has the
  specialist SUSE/Linux-management knowledge for that work. For infrastructure,
  favor work higher up the stack, such as Kubernetes, Docker, and platform
  engineering. General Linux experience does not establish specialist server
  administration expertise or make those projects a good recommendation.
- Assume Philip and Georg can learn unfamiliar backend APIs and data models
  when documentation is publicly available or accessible for the project. Flag
  gaps and ask targeted questions rather than requiring an exact prior reference.
  Exclude genuinely non-negotiable specialist requirements they cannot meet.
  Never claim experience they do not have.
- Philip does not have extensive lakehouse implementation experience or concrete
  implementation experience with Power BI, Metabase, or Databricks. Dashboard
  work itself can be suitable, but it does not establish lakehouse expertise.
  Exclude projects with strong mandatory requirements for extensive lakehouse
  experience rather than presenting them as conditional recommendations based
  only on adjacent data-engineering or dashboard skills.
- Be more flexible about unfamiliar technology stacks in early-stage startup and
  small-company delivery projects than in large enterprise contracts. Philip and
  Georg can get into a small company's stack quickly. An exact stack match is
  not necessary for strong fit, but explicitly required production references
  remain an application caveat; never invent them or assume they can be waived.
- Exclude actual marketing work, including technical-marketer/GTM roles that own
  campaigns, content, prospect-database scraping, lead generation, or outbound
  execution. A close technical stack does not offset this exclusion. Building
  software for marketers to use is acceptable when the engagement is engineering
  delivery rather than responsibility for doing the marketing.
- Exclude engagements requiring fluent or professional French. French-language
  requirements are a hard blocker, including for otherwise attractive technical
  assessments; do not confuse a French-written listing with such a requirement.
- Favor substantive projects with an AI component when they also strengthen
  Isselthal's portfolio.
- Exclude low-value AI-output review, rating, annotation, improvement, and
  AI-training-data or low-level content tasks. Substantive, well-paid AI-lab
  projects remain eligible.
- Exclude opportunities requiring an AI interview or an extensive, multi-round
  job-style interview process.
- Exclude developer-relations and developer-advocacy work.
- Strongly deprioritize past-start listings unless current evidence confirms the
  engagement remains open.
- Check application status before presenting new leads. Do not present already
  sent applications as new opportunities. Skip known applications without
  rechecking their listings or including them as notable findings, unless Philip
  asks to revisit them.
